# Lightflow Studio Development Chronicle

**AI-authored draft for factual and editorial acceptance.** LF-ANY-DOC-020 prepared this record of a human-directed, AI-assisted software project. It is written in third person; it does not speak for Jeremy Running or reconstruct private thoughts and dialogue.

## Scope and reading conventions

This first edition covers **July 20–October 12, 2026, inclusive, in America/Los_Angeles**. All dated headings and release publication times use Pacific daylight time (UTC−07:00). October 12 is still in progress at preparation: this edition records evidence available at the research checkpoint, not a claim to have observed the remainder of that day. Later acceptance or merges must not be projected backward. The accepted main snapshot is `d1531a2f3d2caf282e1215b53615978a6ace5cd1`; separately identified Draft branches are evidence of pending work.

The chronology is selective. Entries group related changes into milestones rather than enumerate every commit. Dates normally identify GitHub merge events, not the first proposal, implementation start, successful run or owner acceptance. Where those differ, the text says so. A merged source change establishes repository delivery; it does not independently reproduce its runtime behavior. Published releases provide a separate distribution boundary.

Evidence labels distinguish **record** (commits, merge/publication metadata and inspected snapshots), **report** (contemporary implementation or validation claims), **conversation summary** (recovered, indirect accounts), **retrospective** (later reconciliation), **interpretation** (editorial reading), and **unknown**. These are claim-level distinctions: a contemporary PR can verify its branch name while only reporting its test results. The [source index](SOURCES.md) gives immutable merge snapshots, dates and limitations. References such as **P243** and **I299** identify entries there. Historical research documents retain their original pending or failed checkpoints; subsequent acceptance supersedes a disposition only within its stated scope.

### September 24 — camera-profile discovery is abandoned

**Attributed decision and report.** Issue #299 records the final product disposition: abandon ExifTool/camera-profile discovery and salvage low-cost FFprobe improvements. Research had found technical possibilities, but owner real-library testing did not establish sufficient cross-camera value. The recorded provider study added no normalized Profile/Gamut facts for 168 decoded clips in the tested camera families. That is bounded provider coverage, not proof that original cameras never store useful metadata. Dependency, licensing, packaging and runtime burden were not justified by the observed benefit; the experimental implementation was rejected, not merged as accepted work. [I299](https://github.com/jeremyrunningphotography/lightflow-studio/issues/299).

The decision deliberately avoided accumulating vendor parsers around a weak product result. #311 received dependency-free technical normalization, and #312 received the separately reproduced pre-existing rotation crash. Automatic Camera/Profile rules #149 remained a product question; closing #299 did not fulfill its original Definition of Done or unlock those rules. This preserves an abandoned approach and useful salvage without turning rejection into a character judgment. [I311](https://github.com/jeremyrunningphotography/lightflow-studio/issues/311), [I149](https://github.com/jeremyrunningphotography/lightflow-studio/issues/149).
