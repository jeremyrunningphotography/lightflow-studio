## July: a toolkit takes shape around concrete workflows

The earliest recovered context for Lightflow Studio is a media workflow that did not behave as expected. On July 20–21, conversation summaries describe Kyno exports failing across several variations. Removing a DJI-supplied LUT reportedly allowed an export, while applying it failed even though preview worked. An assistant diagnostic suggested an internal protocol and threading problem, but the accepted research did not independently establish that explanation. The observed workflow difficulty and the suggested technical cause must remain separate. July 20 is the earliest discussion recovered, not a proven date for the first inspiration. [O1–O3](SOURCES.md#origins-and-repository-anchors)

The recovered July 22 sequence broadened the problem. Damaged or recovered clips produced AAC errors; a Premiere export was described as usable, though with glitches. The user accepted Normal, Salvage and Video-only recovery choices and asked for separate files from clips on a single Premiere timeline. These were related preparation and processing tasks, and the summary records acceptance of a broader Jeremy Media Toolkit. It then records a choice of native Windows WPF without a Python runtime. Those are attributed user decisions in an indirect historical account, rather than quotations or independently reproduced media results. [O1, O2](SOURCES.md#origins-and-repository-anchors)

The assistant reported delivering two ZIPs that afternoon: a Python/FFmpeg v0.1 Toolkit and a WPF v0.2 replacement. The latter was explicitly described as not compile-tested. Neither ZIP was inspected or shown to run in the accepted research. A stronger evidence boundary arrives at 5:39 p.m. PDT, when the first Git commit records WPF v0.2 source. Its batch encoding, LUT selection, recovery modes, metadata inspection, verification, rewrap, proxies, contact sheets and experimental Premiere helper establish the committed utility's scope. They do not retroactively verify the earlier downloads. GitHub repository creation falls on July 23 in UTC, but July 22 in Pacific time, explaining the two dates in earlier descriptions. [G1, G4, O1](SOURCES.md#origins-and-repository-anchors)

That merge dates the application rename, while the repository's own rename remains undated. A verified Windows release pipeline also entered the source. Packaging machinery made distribution possible, but does not prove that a public release accompanied every early version. [G2](SOURCES.md#origins-and-repository-anchors); [P1–P5, P12](SOURCES.md#pull-requests-and-immutable-source-snapshots)

## August: preserving decisions about media

The surviving record next shows substantial playback and processing work on August 9–10. Reusable Flyleaf playback, persistent trim editing and frame-accurate range encoding joined a typed Jobs model and durable Encoding History. Review & Rerun kept an execution record separate from a fresh operation. Partial-output protection prevented unfinished encodes from being presented as completed files before validation. Trim regression fixes in the same sequence show that implementation and correction developed together. [P56–P68](SOURCES.md#pull-requests-and-immutable-source-snapshots)

The first surviving application release, v0.15.0, was published August 10 at 3:54 p.m. PDT. Its notes frame it as a standalone utility checkpoint before the folder-first Browser and Catalog expansion. This is a distribution milestone supported by publication metadata, unlike the early ZIP claims or version proposals. The gaps between surviving tags do not establish missing public releases. [R015](SOURCES.md#release-records)

The most consequential August decision was about what the application should remember. Catalog identity and authored intent were treated as precious data; Previews and derived technical metadata were rebuildable. A thumbnail could be regenerated from a source. A user's organization or review decision could not safely be treated the same way. The distinction gave the expanding application a durable center and constrained future maintenance, recovery and portability. [P84](SOURCES.md#pull-requests-and-immutable-source-snapshots)

The selected architecture used local, single-process SQLite with explicit transactions, forward migrations, foreign keys and stronger Catalog durability. The architecture decision itself did not implement a database: schema, storage configuration, identity and recovery arrived through subsequent PRs. Logical Media Roots and machine-specific mappings separated media identity from a particular computer's path. Stable Assets and source fingerprints allowed technical observations to change without making each path change a new authored object. This became the foundation for keeping organization attached to media across later relocation work. [P85–P89](SOURCES.md#pull-requests-and-immutable-source-snapshots)

Discovery and derived work were built around that separation. Asynchronous enumeration found files; explicit reconciliation maintained Catalog knowledge; background work generated normalized metadata and previews. Filesystem watchers supplied hints, with authoritative refresh as the fallback. Observation of a folder therefore had a different responsibility from ownership of a user's decisions. Separate Preview persistence and cache maintenance made rebuilding disposable data a normal operation rather than a threat to the Catalog. [P94–P105](SOURCES.md#pull-requests-and-immutable-source-snapshots)

The Browser then gave those systems a daily working surface. Folder navigation, a virtualized thumbnail grid, selection, search, filtering, sorting and recursive scope developed within a dark application shell during August 16–19. Browser-integrated Player and Viewer presentation followed on August 21. Persistent In/Out ranges, screengrabs and authored tile state connected viewing with preparation. Lightflow was becoming a place to inspect and decide what to do with media before processing it. A packaged shutdown deadlock corrected during this period also demonstrates why that broader surface needed lifecycle reliability alongside new controls. [P114–P143](SOURCES.md#pull-requests-and-immutable-source-snapshots)

Color workflows extended the same idea. The original utility had selected a LUT for batch output. The August architecture made Camera and Creative assignments durable Catalog intent, consumed by live Player preview, Encoding and Browser Previews through their own rendering paths. Persistent bypass made a review choice explicit. Shared authored intent did not establish calibrated parity across platforms, but it changed Color from an isolated processing option into part of the preparation record. [P151–P164](SOURCES.md#pull-requests-and-immutable-source-snapshots)

Export also changed its place in the product. A focused modal, collision-safe output planning, a global per-file scheduler and Jobs management replaced the dedicated Encoding/Export workspace with a Browser/Player → Export → Jobs flow. The durable boundary was between immutable per-file intent and the live owner of queue execution. A user could prepare media in one context and submit work without making that context responsible for the scheduler. Submission review and safer defaults were revised before the old workspace was retired. [P173–P189](SOURCES.md#pull-requests-and-immutable-source-snapshots)

Named Subclips completed another step from utility toward workbench. Saved ranges referred to source media rather than duplicating it, and could be reviewed, managed and materialized through Export. Corrections to crossing In/Out boundaries, names and creation behavior refined the initial implementation. The Catalog now held decisions about portions of a clip as well as whole-file identity. [P191–P200](SOURCES.md#pull-requests-and-immutable-source-snapshots)

The development process was becoming more explicit alongside the product. Issue-linked slices made ownership visible; tracked AGENTS.md instructions appeared in August; roadmap hygiene became documented delivery work by August 28. Keeping issues, Epics and Project status synchronized preserved the difference between an implemented slice and a broader capability still in progress. These were recorded additions to the workflow, not rules that can be assumed for every earlier branch. [G3](SOURCES.md#origins-and-repository-anchors); [P202](SOURCES.md#pull-requests-and-immutable-source-snapshots)

## September: a coherent review and organization workbench

September's organization features gave practical expression to the precious-data decision. Collections and Collection Sets stored deliberate membership and hierarchy; ratings, flags, labels, keywords and descriptions captured authored assessment. Technical metadata remained derived evidence about a file. A paused frame could become a durable Browser Preview, making the media's representation another explicit choice. Smart Collections later reused Browser queries to define membership from source-backed criteria, while temporary filtering remained a refinement of the current view. [P211–P222, P240, P310](SOURCES.md#pull-requests-and-immutable-source-snapshots)

The interface also consolidated. Inspector information, Subclips and compact Jobs activity converged on a contextual Right Panel instead of competing side surfaces. Browser/Player restoration across relaunch, a context-preserving filmstrip and a sortable Details view made continuity part of review. Timeline markers and non-destructive rotation added preparation state without rewriting original sources; Visual Index navigation offered a way into footage by timestamp. These features changed how users could return to and work through a library, rather than simply enlarging the list of processing commands. [P236–P251, P280, P301, P303](SOURCES.md#pull-requests-and-immutable-source-snapshots)

v0.39.0, published September 7 at 12:31 a.m. PDT, recorded the broad Catalog, Browser and review expansion since v0.15.0. Explorer-style file operations and explicit Export destination choices had joined the workbench. The release jump marks two surviving publication boundaries; it does not authorize a reconstruction of every intervening version as a release. [R039](SOURCES.md#release-records); [P228–P232](SOURCES.md#pull-requests-and-immutable-source-snapshots)

Growing capability exposed performance and lifecycle problems. September 9's Browser revision let known Catalog media appear before asynchronous filesystem reconciliation, with visible checking state and protections against stale work. This improved the order in which useful information became available while retaining authoritative reconciliation. Its reported synthetic timings were evidence for a bounded workload, not a general promise about real libraries. Subsequent LUT refresh churn, Preview navigation deadlock and loading-feedback fixes show that responsiveness remained a series of investigations. [P243, P263, P307](SOURCES.md#pull-requests-and-immutable-source-snapshots); [D2](SOURCES.md#accepted-document-snapshots)

Startup backups and shutdown received similar attention. The September 16 correction reported substantial improvements on matched workloads, but a lasting process change was equally significant: the package smoke test began treating graceful-exit timeout as a failure instead of accepting forced cleanup as success. A build that could open but not close cleanly no longer met that validation outcome. Explicit backup-on-exit later drained authored mutations, while long-path protection and bounded clean startup addressed other parts of Catalog safety. [P266, P306, P319, P341](SOURCES.md#pull-requests-and-immutable-source-snapshots)

Premiere integration grew from the initial experimental helper into an accepted architecture and production handoff. Jeremy selected a modern UXP companion using an authenticated loopback bridge, Catalog identities, and one-way reconciliation with conflict detection. Research established feasibility before separate source-media and native Subclip implementations merged September 16–17. Point-marker projection followed September 21. Lightflow's preparation state could now carry into an editing application through a defined handoff, while the prototype and the first Toolkit helper remained distinct historical stages. [P262, P264, P268, P300](SOURCES.md#pull-requests-and-immutable-source-snapshots)

v0.40.0 was published September 17 at 6:20 p.m. PDT with the smoother Browser/Player, contextual details and Premiere handoff. Its early-stage wording invited hands-on feedback. Publication followed a shared-menu CI blocker and a corrected unpublished candidate tag; the release record preserves the abandoned candidate separately from the accepted source. Maintenance-branch policy then formalized main as the development trunk and released minor lines as patch targets. Distribution, validated source and ongoing development thus had explicit boundaries. [R040](SOURCES.md#release-records); [P275–P283](SOURCES.md#pull-requests-and-immutable-source-snapshots); [D3](SOURCES.md#accepted-document-snapshots)

One consequential September decision reduced scope. Camera-profile discovery had investigated ExifTool and related approaches, but owner real-library testing did not show enough value across the tested cameras. The reported provider study added no normalized Profile/Gamut facts for 168 decoded clips in that bounded corpus. Dependency, licensing, packaging and runtime costs were not justified by the observed benefit. The experimental direction was abandoned on September 24 rather than merged as accepted functionality. Useful dependency-free FFprobe normalization and a separately reproduced rotation crash were handled independently. Automatic camera/profile LUT rules remained a product question. [I299, I311, I149](SOURCES.md#issue-and-owner-decision-records)

This rejection is part of the project's development, without requiring a dramatic interpretation. Research could identify technical possibilities while hands-on review found insufficient product value. The disposition preserved useful findings and an unresolved product question, rather than declaring the original goal completed. It also illustrates why an agent's implementation report could not substitute for Jeremy's acceptance.

The durable October plan selected Apple Silicon, shared Avalonia presentation, neutral C# domain/application logic and narrow native adapters, while continuing to ship WPF during incremental migration. [P371, P381](SOURCES.md#pull-requests-and-immutable-source-snapshots); [I366, I367](SOURCES.md#issue-and-owner-decision-records)

Player, Catalog and UI/image proofs advanced through revisions rather than a single all-platform success. Catalog research also revised direction. A central active NAS Catalog was considered, but the selected boundary became supported local active SQLite plus verified closed transfer. [P408, P403](SOURCES.md#pull-requests-and-immutable-source-snapshots)

Closed-Catalog transfer reused lifecycle, mutation, recovery and configuration ownership. Manifests, verified staging and capability-dependent publication protected identity and retained source data. ## Maintaining the record

Historical maintenance follows meaningful developments, rather than every commit. A major feature, published release, consequential architectural or product decision, abandoned direction, important safety qualification or change in AI development practice is a candidate. The completing agent should identify the candidate in its final report with a concise factual summary and issue, PR and commit references. Routine fixes, UI adjustments, CI reruns and registry changes normally need no entry unless they represent a larger historical decision.

In a dedicated follow-up, append a selective dated Chronicle entry and extend the Source Index with stable references, immutable source anchors and evidence limits. Identify whether the date describes a decision, merge, acceptance or publication. Preserve the distinction between a report of behavior and verified delivery, and distinguish contemporary records from recovered summaries or later reconciliation. Future editions must state their own cutoff; later events must not be projected into an earlier accepted snapshot.

Revise this longer History periodically when accumulated Chronicle material changes the meaningful account of a phase or warrants a new one. It remains derived from the Chronicle and sources; one new milestone need not trigger a full narrative revision. For a correction, explicitly identify the earlier claim, the newly established evidence and the correction date, preserving the earlier edition's provenance. Update affected prose transparently rather than silently substituting today's state for yesterday's.

Both documents remain explicitly AI-authored. Do not invent personal motivations or publish private transcripts or sensitive evidence without explicit owner review. Historical writing may be batched and adds no production merge or release gate: a missing entry does not invalidate accepted production work. The [repository policy](../../AGENTS.md#historical-documentation-maintenance) records this practice without a new registry or mandatory approval system.
